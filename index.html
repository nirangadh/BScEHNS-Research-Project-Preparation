Research Project Preparation

This module helps you plan your final-year research project. You do not build the project here. You plan it so clearly that you can build it in the next module, NB6019CEM Research Project Delivery.

In this page you will learn
  • what the module asks you to produce, and when
  • why one word, framing, runs through every session
  • where to find each session's notes and practice tools

You are already a capable builder. This module adds a different skill: turning an interesting topic into a question that a project can actually answer. That skill is called framing, and every session works on it from a new direction.

The first three weeks are called seeding. Seeding means finding an idea worth researching and giving it a first frame, so that by the Week 3 checkpoint a broad starting idea has become a first research question. The seeding document, in Week 1, is where you begin.

Each session has a notes page on this site. The notes use the same words your lecturer uses in class, so you can read them before or after the session. Around them you will find short explanations, practice questions with answers, and small tools to try ideas out.

A picture to think with: framing
The picture
A frame stands in front of a busy scene. Whatever is inside the frame is sharp and becomes the picture. Whatever is outside it fades and is left out.
Why it works
A research question does the same job. It decides what your project will look at and measure, and what it will leave out. A good frame makes the picture clear; a good question makes the project testable.
Where it stops working
A frame on an easel stays where you put it. Your question does not: you are expected to improve it, at the Week 2 clinic and after the Week 3 checkpoint. Treat your first question as a draft.

Why this is assessed

Your proposal is the coursework for this module (CW1). It is marked against five criteria, one for each part of a strong proposal, and each session teaches towards at least one of them. After you submit, you defend the proposal in Week 13. The module map shows how the weeks fit together. Open the module map.

Words to know

WordWhat it means
ProposalA plan for a research project: the problem, the question, what others have already found, how you will test your idea, and how you will work safely and legally.
Research questionOne question that your project is designed to answer. A good one names what you will build or test, and how you will know whether it worked.
SeedingThe first stage of your project, Weeks 1 to 3: finding an idea worth researching and giving it a first frame. It ends at the Week 3 checkpoint.
CheckpointA point in the term where you submit work and receive feedback. This module has three.
SupervisionMeetings with your supervisor between the taught sessions, to work on your own proposal.
