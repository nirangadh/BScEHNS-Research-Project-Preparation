Research Question Self-Check

[Illustrative example - do not cite] The example questions in this tool belong to invented students.

One idea

A strong question meets five dimensions at once. This tool helps you to see which one your draft misses first.

Not a grader

This tool is a thinking mirror, not an automated grader. It surfaces gaps for you to reflect on - a green light is not a guarantee of a strong question. Bring your question to the Week 2 clinic regardless of the result. All data stays in this browser tab; nothing is sent anywhere.

A

Specific - does the question name technique, threat, and system?

not yet rated ▼

A specific research question names the technique (not just a category), the threat class or problem type, and the system or protocol it addresses. "Machine learning" is a category covering hundreds of approaches. "A tree-based classifier trained on flow-level features" is specific. "Intrusion" is a category. "Lateral-movement traffic in an enterprise LAN" is specific.

Heuristic hint: if your question contains generic terms like "machine learning", "AI", "deep learning", "data science", or "intrusion detection" without naming a specific algorithm, model type, or threat sub-class, it is likely still at the topic level.
Possible gap detected: your question may contain a generic category term without a specific named technique or approach. Consider pinning the exact algorithm, method, or tool you will use.
B

Technically grounded - would a security practitioner recognise this language?

not yet rated ▼

The question should use language that a security practitioner would recognise - specific protocols, attack techniques, detection approaches, configuration standards. If the question could have been written by someone with no security expertise, it is probably not technically grounded. Value words like "good", "better", "effective", or "secure" without a defined measure are the most common symptom of this failure.

Heuristic hint: flag any of these words in your question: "good", "better", "best", "effective", "efficient", "secure", "safe", "optimal". Each one should be replaced with a named, measurable technical criterion.
Possible gap detected: your question may contain a value word that is not defined in measurable technical terms. Replace it with a specific metric or property.
C

Achievable at Level 6 - can this be built and tested in a self-built testbed?

not yet rated ▼

The question must be evaluable in a self-built virtualised or laboratory environment within the project timeline. Nothing in the question should require access to a live production system, a third-party network you are not authorised to test, or classified data. Words like "prevent", "eliminate", or "all organisations" are almost always red flags at Level 6; they imply a scope or certainty that is not achievable.

Heuristic hint: flag any of these patterns: "prevent", "eliminate", "completely", "all hospitals", "all universities", "all organisations", "the internet". Also flag: testing against "a real company", "the university network", or "live systems" without mention of authorisation, a lab, or a testbed.
Possible gap detected: your question may contain an absolute term ("prevent", "eliminate") or an unscoped population. Consider replacing it with a bounded testbed environment and a directional claim ("reduce", "detect").
D

Aims differentiated from objectives - one aim, distinct measurable objectives

not yet rated ▼

This dimension cannot be assessed from the research question alone - it requires checking your separate aims and objectives section. An aim is the single overarching outcome the project is working toward. Objectives are the differentiated, measurable steps that together deliver the aim. The most common error: listing three sentences all phrased "to investigate X", "to examine Y", "to explore Z" - that is one aim stated three times, not three objectives.

Check your aims and objectives document: (1) Do you have exactly one aim? (2) Do your objectives each describe a different action? (3) Could each objective be completed independently, and does completing all of them deliver the aim? If your objectives all use the same verb ("to investigate..."), that is a warning sign.
E

Evaluable - metric, baseline, and environment all present?

not yet rated ▼

An evaluable question includes three things: a measurable outcome (detection rate, false-positive rate, attack success rate, login completion time, hosts encrypted...), a comparison or baseline ("compared with", "versus", "relative to", "against a baseline of..."), and a defined environment (a testbed, a lab, a VM environment, a controlled network segment). Without all three, there is no way to assess whether the result is good, bad, or expected.

Heuristic hint: does your question contain: (1) a measurable term (rate, percentage, time, count, accuracy, precision, recall)? (2) a comparison phrase ("compared with", "versus", "relative to")? (3) an environment reference (testbed, lab, VM, VLAN, controlled environment)? If any of these is missing, it is the first thing to add.
Possible gap detected: your question may be missing a comparison/baseline, a measurable outcome, or an environment reference. Try adding "compared with [alternative or baseline]" and naming the testbed environment.
