One idea

A paragraph of a review either describes its sources or engages with them. This tool shows you the signs of each in your own paragraph, and names one edit to try first.

Not a grader

This is a thinking mirror, not a grader. A positive signal does not guarantee a high mark, and a negative one does not mean your writing is beyond repair. Use it to sharpen your paragraphs before the Week 8 draft.

Reference exemplar - Tharindu's credential-stuffing question
Illustrative example - do not cite
Detection approaches for credential-stuffing divide broadly into threshold-based and feature-based methods, and the division matters for a SOC that must keep false positives low. Threshold methods such as IP rate-limiting (Perera, 2021) are cheap to deploy but degrade against distributed attacks that stay under per-IP limits, which is the precise case a mobile-banking endpoint faces. Feature-based classifiers (Silva and Fernando, 2022; Jayawardena, 2023) address distribution by scoring behaviour rather than volume, but both evaluate on web traffic with stable client fingerprints, whereas mobile clients churn device and network identifiers in ways that may inflate false positives. No reviewed study evaluates feature-based detection on mobile-banking authentication specifically, and none reports a false-positive rate against a triage threshold. That absence is the gap this project occupies, and it is what the research question's comparison against a rate-limiting baseline is designed to test.
Four structural moves visible here: (1) grouping - threshold vs feature-based, not source-by-source; (2) contrast - "cheap to deploy but degrade"; (3) named limitation - "mobile clients churn device and network identifiers"; (4) gap wired to the question - "that absence is the gap... the research question is designed to test."
